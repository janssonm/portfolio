01 Evaluating Minkowski pooling in CGVQM for video quality
assessment of modern rendering distortions

KTH, 2026

Links
Read the thesis (PDF) ↗

Modern rendering techniques such as path tracing, neural supersampling, and Gaussian splatting introduce spatio-temporal distortions that traditional video quality metrics predict poorly. The recently proposed Computer Graphics Video Quality Metric (CGVQM) by Intel addresses this by measuring quality in the feature space of a pre-trained 3D convolutional neural network, but it aggregates its local feature differences into a final score using mean pooling, which weights every region equally.

I replaced the mean pooling with Minkowski pooling, which puts more weight on the stronger local distortions. I varied its exponent over six values, p ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8}, re-calibrated the metric for each on three video quality datasets, and evaluated every variant against human ratings on the CG-VQD dataset of modern rendering distortions.

Result

0.820 → 0.873

Agreement with human ratings (PLCC). My results show that implementing Minkowski pooling improves agreement with human ratings of modern rendering distortions.

Trombone Trolley is a VR music game made in the course DH2413 Advanced Graphics and Interaction. I was responsible for the environment design.

I created the game map using a mix of existing assets and custom-made 3D models made in Blender, and implemented a procedural generation system to support scalability and future expansion. I also developed a custom toon shader to control the scene's lighting and visual atmosphere.

Result

In a few weeks, our team built a VR game for the course exhibition. Good communication kept us working well together, and I gained a deeper understanding of graphics and game logic in Unity.

03 Procedural generation of forests using L-systems and Poisson-disk sampling

This project was done as part of my very first computer graphics course. My project partner and I combined two techniques to generate forests in Unity. The Poisson-disk sampling algorithm is used to place trees semi-randomly, and L-systems grow each tree from a set of rewriting rules.

We built the project on a Poisson-disk sampling tutorial by Sebastian Lague and an open-source L-system tree generator, and connected the two. Spacing, branching angle, segment length and growth iterations can be adjusted in the implementation.

Result

This is where I learned the basics of computer graphics, and found out how fun it is to turn math into visuals.
